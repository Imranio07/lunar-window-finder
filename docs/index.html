★ best-lit spots of the Artemis III regions (outlines in white) · ● real missions · ○ your point. Up is the direction of longitude 0°, the side that faces Earth.

How it works here: the terrain is a 240 m version of the LOLA map, read in your browser; the horizon is found with the same 720 rays. The Sun and Earth directions are taken from the nearest computed site and turned to your point (for Earth with a parallax correction; tested against JPL Horizons at other sites: within 0.04°). Anywhere else on the Moon you can type coordinates: the point is then computed on a smooth Moon.

This is a quick look. At 19 sites computed both ways, the 240 m result differs from the 80 m calculation by 2.4 percentage points of sunlight on average, and by up to 15 where a ridge stands within a few hundred metres of the point.
